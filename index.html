[ 02 ]  More than a dock

A dock charges. A berth runs the operation.

Lands drones from many makers

An open landing and charging interface for PX4 and ArduPilot drones. The station's own camera guides the final approach over MAVLink.

Inspects every aircraft

Cameras in the bay photograph props, arms and landing gear after each flight and flag damage before it turns into a failure.

Keeps the logbook

Every flight is recorded and signed at the station: route, weather, battery and inspection results. Ready for waivers today and BVLOS record keeping tomorrow.

Watches the sky

ADS-B and Remote ID receivers on the mast listen for nearby aircraft and drones, and a weather station decides when it is safe to launch.

Stores and processes data on site

Imagery lands on the station's own drive and is processed at the edge, so smaller drones can carry less computer and results arrive faster.

Navigates without GPS

Each station builds and refreshes a survey-grade map of its site. Drones download it before takeoff and can hold position when GPS is jammed or lost.

[ 02.1 ]  The flight record

Proof of every flight, signed where it happened.

The station signs each record with its own key before anything leaves the site. Operators, insurers and auditors can check that nothing was changed later. Inspection photos go to the drone maker only when the operator opts in.

[ 04 ]  For drone makers

Your drone. Our ground.

Many compliant US drones have no dock of their own, and building one costs a drone maker years. Berth publishes an open landing and charging interface, so a maker can ship a docked product without building the ground side.

  1. Step 1

    Fit the kit

    Charging contacts and a landing marker on the landing gear. No change to the flight controller.

  2. Step 2

    Add a profile

    Size, battery chemistry, charge curve and landing offsets, stored as one drone profile.

  3. Step 3

    Qualify it

    Hundreds of landings in simulation and on a hardware-in-the-loop rig before any field unit ships.

  4. Step 4

    Ship it docked

    The maker's customers get a station, signed records and fleet software on day one.

[ 05 ]  Why now

The US drone fleet is being rebuilt. Its ground hasn't been.

Supply
Dec 22, 2025

The FCC added foreign-made drones and key components to its Covered List. New models can't get authorization. Most third-party docks were built around those drones, so the new US-built fleet needs a new ground system.

Source: DSLRPros, FCC update
Regulation
Part 108

The FAA's rule for routine flights beyond visual line of sight was proposed in August 2025 and has been in final White House review since July 2026. It rewards automation, record keeping and awareness of nearby aircraft.

Source: DLA Piper, Part 108
Demand
1,000+

The leading US single-brand dock has passed a thousand installations. Buyers already pay for drones that live on site. Each of those docks still serves only one brand of drone.

Source: Robotics Press

[ 07 ]  Roadmap

From one bench rig to a ground network.

  1. Q4 2026 – Q1 2027

    Bench prototype

    Precision landing of a PX4 quad to ±5 cm, 20 times in a row, with contact charging and the first signed records.

  2. 2027

    Alpha stations

    3–5 field units at US industrial sites. First drone-maker integration. Inspection cameras and sky awareness online.

  3. 2028

    Pilot fleet

    50+ stations with 2–3 drone makers. Records built for Part 108 operations. Managed plan for sites without a drone team.

  4. 2029 +

    The network

    Swap-ready power bay, shared scheduling across sites, station-built maps for GPS-free flight, and stations as local positioning beacons.
